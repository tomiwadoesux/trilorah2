## 1. Standard verse references and voice-command pills

The requested verse labels were **John 3:16** and **Acts 2:3**, rather than the raw spoken wording such as “John three sixteen.”

Implemented:

- Complete references are checked against the Bible database before forming a pill.
- Confirmed references use standard book, chapter and verse formatting, including ranges such as **Romans 8:28-30**.
- Recognized voice commands also form animated pills with their action icons.
- Translation commands use the same Bible icon as scripture references, with a compact translation label such as **KJV**.
- Command pills are driven by actual engine events. Ordinary speech containing similar words does not automatically become a command pill.
- Pills remain stable while partial speech is revised and finalized, and appear in the expanded transcript too.
- The original speech is retained. Transcript search accepts both the original wording and the displayed reference or command label.
- Fast navigation now reports its command to the transcript; its final ASR result is consumed before it can advance the verse a second time.

The icons use the app's existing Solar Bold Duotone family:

| --- | --- | --- |
| “John three sixteen” | John 3:16 | Bible |
| “King James Version” | KJV | Bible |

A requested translation that is not installed stays plain rather than appearing as a successful switch. “Amen” outside prayer mode does not form a resume pill.

## 2. Use the sandbox's yellow linear gradient at low opacity

The requested colour was the existing sandbox linear-gradient material, using its yellow variant with low opacity.

Implemented:

- All confirmed verse and voice-command pills use the shared yellow surface recipe.
- The gradient uses the existing four colour stops and **48-degree** control angle.
- Resting fill opacity is **8%**. Text and icons remain readable at their normal text opacity.
- Confirmation produces a subtle **520 ms** pulse: the gradient's middle briefly reaches 14% opacity and its edges 12%, then both settle at 8%.
- Pill corners remain **5 px**, preserving the softly squared shape.

This establishes yellow as the common pill colour; the distinct icons identify each command.
