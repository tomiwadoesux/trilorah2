# Teach Trilorah your app in five clicks

Trilorah can press buttons in another program. The obvious next question is how it knows which button is *Go live* in yours. The answer is that you show it, once, and it takes about two minutes.

## Five questions, one click each

In the console, under **screen**, choose **+ teach an app**. Trilorah lists the programs open on the computer. Pick yours. It then asks five questions, one at a time, in big type:

1. *Show me how you search for a song or verse in [your app] — click the search box.*
2. *Show me how you put what's selected on the screen — click that button.*
3. *Show me how you take it off the screen — click clear or black.*
4. *Show me next.*
5. *Show me previous.*

For each one, you click the real control in your app. Trilorah is watching, and the moment you click it says *✓ got it — the button "Go Live"* and moves to the next question. If your app has no search, there is a **skip** button, and it says so in plain words. Only question two is required: without it, Trilorah cannot put anything on your screen, and the flow tells you that rather than carrying on.

What it records is not where your mouse was. It records *which element* you clicked — its name, its type, its place in the app's structure — so it still finds the button after the window has been moved, resized or reopened on a different monitor.

## Then it checks its own homework

When the questions are done, Trilorah looks up every control it was just taught, without pressing any of them, and tells you the result: *TextEdit is ready ✓*, or a list of what it could not find again. Your app appears under **screen** beside Trilorah's own wall. Tap it, and Go live, Clear, Next and Previous now land in your program.

## Do this at sound check, not during a service

Teaching is a Saturday job. It needs you to click things in the presentation app, which is not something anyone wants to be doing while the congregation can see the projector. Trilorah does not learn an app during a service; if a program is unmapped, it says so and keeps its hands to itself.

## The apps we'll teach for you

For the software churches most often run, the plan is that we record these answers ourselves and ship them inside Trilorah, so most teams never see the five questions: their app simply appears under **screen** on a computer that has it. EasyWorship is first on that list, and it is on the list rather than in the product today — it runs only on Windows, and we have not yet recorded it there. The last piece in this series says exactly where that stands.

A taught recipe on your computer always wins over one we shipped. Your church's screen beats our guess about it.
