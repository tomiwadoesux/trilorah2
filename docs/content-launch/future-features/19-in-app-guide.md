# Show me where.

“Open the settings” leaves quite a lot of work to the person who has never seen them.

Trilorah's in-app guide can take you to supported controls, point at the next step, and demonstrate a click or type the words you supply. Open **i guide** at the top right, find a task, and choose **Show me** or **I'll do it**.

The moving triangle shows where the action happens. A short caption explains the next step. You can watch a demonstrated action, click the control yourself, or take over and have the guide wait.

## A useful interruption

Suppose you're preparing an announcement and want to find an online image of a sunrise. Give the guide your search words and choose the online-image task.

If this computer has no saved Pixabay or Pexels key, the guide can point you to the relevant settings and keep your search ready. You enter and save the key yourself. When the required setup is present, return to the search and continue.

The key still has to work with its provider. Saving it and getting a successful search result are different checks.

Once results appear, you choose the image. The guide doesn't decide which photograph belongs in your service or send it to the room for you.

## Learn one job, then the next

Supported walkthroughs include finding a passage, opening song entry, preparing a service from text, locating media, choosing audio, and reaching screen setup. Some steps can be demonstrated; others deliberately wait for your choice, such as selecting a physical display or a local file.

The guide currently follows prepared workflows. Other mapped topics may offer directions or quick steps rather than a full demonstration. A request outside those workflows won't turn into unrestricted control of the app.

For a first rehearsal, choose one small job and complete it. Then let the next volunteer try **I'll do it** while you watch. Our [volunteer training guide](../articles/14-train-a-new-volunteer.md) gives that practice a complete service to work toward.
