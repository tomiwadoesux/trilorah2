# Five product-writing examples for Trilorah

Research accessed: **11 October 2026**. Audience for the eventual Trilorah writing: church volunteers preparing and running services. Status: research notes; the proposed voice and example lines still require the founder’s choice.

These are five selected examples within a larger ten-brand study, not an objectively ranked list of the best brands. Each brand has an official announcement and an official help or documentation page below. The observations concern these pages, not every piece the company has published. Older announcements remain useful writing examples; their launch claims are not current setup instructions.

Method: read the published page text and its media placements. Videos were not watched frame by frame, and the products were not tested. “Observed” identifies something on the page. “Transfer” is an editorial interpretation for Trilorah. No conversion, engagement, or commercial performance is inferred from the writing.

## Linear: make the situation concrete, then offer an action

**Observed — announcement.** [Introducing Linear Agent](https://linear.app/changelog/2026-03-24-introducing-linear-agent), 24 March 2026. A literal feature title sits above a video and a direct trial action. The opening moves from a product-team problem—finding useful context across work—to the new capability. Longer explanatory sentences alternate with short pivots. Instead of stopping at a capability list, it gives recognizable jobs and example prompts. UI images accompany specific sections. Invocation details, availability, and a separately marked upcoming capability limit ambiguity. The CTA asks the reader to try the product; a linked letter supplies the broader vision.

**Observed — help.** [Start Guide](https://linear.app/docs/start-guide), no publication date shown. This is an orientation hub rather than an exhaustive tutorial. It defines the product briefly, offers a video and an interactive demo, explains the demo’s limits, and routes people by role and team circumstances. Short sections link to the next useful task. Warmth comes from making the starting point manageable, rather than jokes. There is little need for a comparison table or a long numbered procedure here.

**Transfer — inference.** A Trilorah introduction can begin with one familiar service-day problem, name the relevant workflow, show it, and point to one next action. A separate getting-started hub can route volunteers to microphone setup, screens, scripture, or service preparation. It need not teach every feature on one page.

**Do not copy.** Developer-team vocabulary, broad agent promises, or claims that the companion can operate every part of the app. Describe supported guides and their actual controls.

**Optional original tone line, not approved copy:** “The service starts at ten. The software tour does not.”

## Stripe: precision can carry the story

**Observed — announcement.** [Introducing credits for usage-based billing](https://stripe.com/blog/introducing-credits-for-usage-based-billing), 11 February 2025. The literal headline and named author set a matter-of-fact tone. The opening gives several business situations before explaining the feature. Concrete time periods and scenarios make abstract rules easier to follow. Longer sentences accommodate conditions, while bullets organize feature properties. A product image establishes the object being discussed. Future work has its own section. Documentation and an expert conversation are the relevant next actions; the article does not use jokes to manufacture warmth.

**Observed — help.** [Billing credits](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/usage-based/billing-credits), no publication date shown. A diagram explains relationships. A table compares states. Bullets distinguish eligibility conditions, and a numbered sequence explains precedence. Worked examples show what happens in different invoice situations. These formats perform different jobs: the table is useful because readers need to compare states, while ordered steps matter where sequence changes the result. Boundaries remain close to the behavior they qualify.

**Transfer — inference.** Scripture recognition needs the same respect for conditions: what the app heard, what it matched, what the operator sees, and what the operator must do next. A short state table may explain the distinction between a detected passage and projected content more clearly than another persuasive paragraph. A troubleshooting guide should identify an observable check, not just say that setup is easy.

**Do not copy.** Dense financial terminology or implementation detail that gives a volunteer no useful decision. Precision does not require writing like a contract. Do not import the source’s commercial claims into Trilorah copy.

**Optional original tone line, not approved copy:** “The microphone is connected. It would now like to be tested.”

## Vercel: connect the benefit to the mechanism

**Observed — announcement.** [Introducing Fluid compute](https://vercel.com/blog/introducing-fluid-compute), 4 February 2025. The opening establishes a technical tradeoff and introduces the feature as a response. A definition and a short principles list follow. Benefit sections explain named mechanisms; an illustration makes the model easier to compare. The ending gives an exact place to enable the feature and a place to check its effect. Rhythm alternates compact claims with technical explanation. The page includes vendor performance claims; this review does not independently validate them.

**Observed — help.** [Fluid compute](https://vercel.com/docs/fluid-compute), last updated 24 August 2026. The documentation gives current defaults, numbered dashboard instructions, configuration examples, supported runtimes, and operational limits. A diagram explains concurrency; tables compare plan settings and precedence. The help page’s newer defaults show why an announcement should not become the permanent setup manual. Its CTA is essentially task completion plus links to the relevant technical detail.

**Transfer — inference.** For screens, explain the volunteer’s desired result first, then show the routing that produces it. A labeled diagram is useful when it answers “Which screen gets which content?” A setup guide should include a visible check on the destination display. Keep announcement copy and current instructions linked but separately maintained.

**Do not copy.** Developer shorthand, sweeping effortless-setup claims, or unverified savings. Trilorah should earn confidence through a visible result. A short technical noun is not automatically a clear noun to a new volunteer.

**Optional original tone line, not approved copy:** “The stage screen has a job. Let’s brief it.”

## Notion: put the feature inside an ordinary moment

**Observed — announcement.** [Introducing Notion 3.0](https://www.notion.com/blog/introducing-notion-3-0), 18 September 2025. A short summary precedes the longer story. The article moves through a familiar afternoon of unfinished work, a before-and-after product history, and specific multi-step examples. The co-founder’s personal aside adds warmth. Videos, illustrations, and example links make the capability easier to imagine. The prose mixes short declarations with conversational paragraphs and use-case bullets. Upcoming custom agents are separated from the launch and have a signup action. Broad capability claims are part of the source’s marketing, not findings established by this review.

**Observed — help.** [Get started with Notion Agent](https://www.notion.com/help/notion-agent), no publication date shown; the page heading is “Notion Agent.” A video and short introduction precede permissions and usage context. Capability lists repeatedly supply example prompts. A separate limitations section states unsupported actions. Numbered personalization steps and UI screenshots serve specific tasks. Exact controls replace the announcement’s broader storytelling when the reader needs to act. The page can be friendly while still explaining constraints.

**Transfer — inference.** Trilorah can open with a recognizably untidy moment: the service order arriving across several messages, or a last-minute scripture change. Follow it with one concrete workflow and an honest endpoint. For the companion, show one input, one response, and the next choice. A short recording can prove the interaction; a screenshot can label the control.

**Do not copy.** Claims of unlimited agent capability, autonomous work across arbitrary tools, or future behavior presented as available today. A vivid scene should not imply that Trilorah reads volunteers’ messages or imports from services that are not connected.

**Optional original tone line, not approved copy:** “The service plan is in six messages. A bold filing system.”

## Slack: make the human interaction familiar and the instructions short

**Observed — announcement.** [Introducing new ways to work side by side in Slack Huddles](https://slack.com/blog/productivity/introducing-new-ways-to-work-in-slack-huddles), 22 June 2022. This historical launch piece starts with a lengthy workplace context, then becomes more concrete: short feature bullets, familiar work situations, a product image, quotations, and sections about actual interactions. Emoji and casual examples provide warmth. A design review and help from remote colleagues give the functionality a setting. The article explicitly dates the expected rollout and links to its event. Its satisfaction and adoption claims are vendor claims, not evidence that the writing itself performs well.

**Observed — help.** [Use huddles in Slack](https://slack.com/help/articles/4402059015315-Use-huddles-in-Slack), no publication date shown. A brief purpose statement and a screenshot lead into expectations and limits. Desktop and mobile instructions are separated, usually in short numbered sequences. Verbs and exact controls dominate. Tips explain access and feature restrictions near the relevant task. There is no need for a narrative interruption between every click. The main completion actions are functional, with an article-feedback prompt at the end.

**Transfer — inference.** Tell a mobile-control story through a volunteer’s need to check something away from the desk. Once the feature is verified, keep its actual desktop and phone steps separate and show both screens where pairing crosses devices. Familiar situations provide warmth; the setup instructions can remain direct.

**Do not copy.** The long corporate opening, a dense layer of emoji, or imagined customer quotations. Humor should land on the awkward workflow, not the volunteer trying to learn it.

**Optional original tone line for a future, verified mobile-control story; not a release claim:** “You may now leave the booth. The booth will cope.”

## What these examples suggest for the existing drafts

These are recommendations from the comparison, not measured findings about Trilorah’s audience:

| Writing job | Useful form | Visual that earns its place | Application to Trilorah |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Introduce the product | Familiar situation, clear position, concrete capabilities, next action | One overview or short service workflow | Give the launch introduction a point of view; move detailed setup into linked guides. |
| Announce a feature | What changed, who benefits, one example, availability, next action | Short recording of the changed behavior | Write a separate story for a meaningful new workflow, after verifying it. |
| Teach a task | Starting conditions, exact steps, visible result, common failure | Screenshots at genuine decision points | Keep the beginner drafts useful and easy to update. |
| Explain behavior | Inputs, decisions, outcomes, limits | Labeled diagram or state comparison | Especially useful for recognition, projection control, and display routing. |
| Tell a customer story | Real context, actions, observed result, attributed evidence | Permissioned photographs or actual setup | Wait for real customers and permission; do not invent launch testimonials. |

Sixteen articles can supply a sensible initial library, but a count is not a content strategy. Not every control deserves an article. Keep reusable help separate from dated announcements, and reserve feature stories for changes substantial enough to explain a different way of working.

For the requested tone review, the five sample lines above are options only. They do not establish a voice guide or authorize rewrites of the existing articles. Their shared device is a straight-faced observation about an awkward task; the useful instruction would follow immediately.
