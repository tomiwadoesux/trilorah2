# Why I started Trilorah

The first time I joined the operators as a volunteer, I saw the pressure on the person running the screen.

They were queuing verses, trying to work out which passage the preacher was quoting when no reference was given, and putting songs together on the spot. Several jobs needed attention at the same time.

That was the starting point for Trilorah.

Knowing a quotation was only part of the job. The operator still had to find the passage and get the right words onto the screen while the service continued. Preparing a song needed the same attention, at the same desk.

Trilorah is being built around that person.

It brings songs, scripture, slides, media and the order of service into one workspace. You can prepare the material together, check it, and choose when to show it. During a sermon, listening can help find spoken scripture references and recognisable quotations. The operator still reviews the suggestion and decides what reaches the screen.

Those early observations give the features a purpose. Scripture suggestions help with finding the passage. Saved songs and a prepared run give the operator material to work from. Clear guides give the next volunteer somewhere to begin.

There is still work to do, including testing the workflows with the people and equipment that will actually use them. That matters to this product. A feature needs to make sense when someone is trying to run a service, as well as when we demonstrate it.

If you have been the person trying to keep up at the screen, you are the person I want Trilorah to help.
