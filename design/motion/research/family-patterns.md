# Family interaction patterns — the catalogue (frame-measured)

Every pattern in Benji Taylor's "Family Values" essay, measured from the 53 clips at **20 fps (a frame every 50 ms)**, with pixel-row measurements of edges and per-frame opacity estimates. The per-clip evidence is in `docs/design/research/family-interactions.md`. Confidence is high on durations (±50 ms) and medium on distances (±5–10 pt).

Two facts frame everything below:
- **Nothing overshoots.** Across ~2,000 settled frames in 53 clips, no edge, card, digit or glyph ever passed its target and came back. Bounce does not exist in this language; save springs-with-bounce for the rare delight moment, if at all.
- **Everything is faster than it looks.** The first analysis (one frame per 0.1–0.8 s) had most timings 1.5–3× too slow. Real numbers: state flips 100–150 ms, trays 150–200 ms, page steps 200–250 ms, flights 150–250 ms. The ~200–250 ms you *feel* before anything moves is the finger: **every app here fires on touch-up**, with a pressed state shown instantly on touch-down.

Use this file when you're about to animate something: find the pattern, take its choreography and timing, build it with the tokens.

## The universal rules (measured across all clips)

1. **Fire on release, respond on touch-down.** Pressed state (tint or ~0.97 scale) appears the frame the finger lands; the transition starts on touch-up and never adds its own delay (31, 32, 39, 41, 47, 51 all show the same 200–250 ms press then instant motion).
2. **Two easing families, no overshoot.** (a) *Heavy ease-out* — 33–60 % of the travel in the first 50 ms, ~90 % by 100–150 ms: trays born from buttons (08, 11, 12, 27, 49, 50, 51), translate-ins, shared-element flights, cross-slides. (b) *Spring from rest* — ~10 % in the first 50 ms, ~50 % by 100 ms, ~85 % by 150 ms, a 1–2 px creep to 250–300 ms: pure height morphs of an already-open tray (01, 02, 07, 35), some returns (09, 15 card→chip, 34 avatar). Never ease-in on UI. Exits are the same speed or faster, never slower.
3. **Durations cluster by kind, not by size.** State flips (digit, label, check, tab colour, enable) 100–150 ms · tray open 150–200 ms, close 100–150 ms · in-tray step 150–200 ms *whatever the height delta* (a 200 pt change and a 27 pt change take the same 4 frames — velocity scales, duration doesn't: 13, 49 vs 50) · page cross-slide 200–250 ms (incoming title tails to ~300) · shared-element flight 150–250 ms · container transform 200–250 ms · FLIP re-layout 250–300 ms · card journey 250–300 ms · keyboard-coupled moves follow the system keyboard curve (~90 % at 200 ms, settled 350). Delight sequences are the only long things, and they are staged.
4. **Shape first, content later.** Opacity crossfades run 100–150 ms and finish *before or with* the geometry they belong to; they start on the first moving frame (or one frame before, for label morphs). Old content leaves faster than new arrives (~15–25 % vs ~75 % at mid). Secondary motion (an illustration, a tooltip, a subtitle, an arrow) waits 150–400 ms after the primary lands, then runs once.
5. **Trays are full-width from frame one, and only the top edge moves.** The bottom edge and width never change, including when a tray is "born from its button" (08, 11, 12, 27): the slab is at ~97–100 % width in the first motion frame; what animates is the top edge, the CTA rising ~10–16 pt and widening ~6–8 pt into the tray's inset, and the label. Content is pinned to the moving top edge and clipped — often *behind the CTA*, so text appears to slide out from under the button (27, 49, 50). Short trays reveal content opaque (03, 27, 49); tall trays fade it in 30→100 % over the same 150 ms (29), body one frame after the slab and Cancel one frame after that (08).
6. **In-tray steps: new content rides the edge, old content stays put.** The incoming layout is pinned to the moving top edge; the outgoing layout stays fixed in screen space, is clipped by the edge, and fades in ~150 ms — sometimes with a ~1.05 scale and a slight softening (13, 35), usually plain (01, 05, 07). Titles share one slot when the layouts match (02, 04, 05, 07). Heights do *not* have to differ (04's five steps are 416/477/410/408/416 pt): the step reads from the same-slot title crossfade. ✕ on a nested step pops one level; a scrim tap dismisses the whole stack.
7. **Scrim, no blur, no page scale.** Light flows ~27 % black; dark flows 60–68 %. The scrim fades in on the tray's curve (or leads it by a frame) and **lifts ahead of the tray on dismiss** (08, 11, 12, 13, 51). The page behind never moves or scales for in-place trays; it recedes to ~0.92 only for tray→full-screen (09), and that recede *leads* the card. Status bar undimmed.
8. **The CTA is one persistent widget.** It never slides with a page step; it rides the keyboard with a constant gap on the keyboard's own curve (03, 15, 16, 20, 49); disabled is ~35–40 % opacity crossfaded in 100–150 ms; it becomes a tray's CTA by rising ~14 pt; a two-action tray shrinks it into the right half while Cancel fades in a frame later (08, 29). When it changes labels it **morphs**, keeping shared letters/words.
9. **Label morph mechanics (22, 23, 27, 40, 46, 51).** Fades start ~50 ms *before* the slide; shared glyphs slide ~150 ms on an S-curve (nothing in the first 50 ms, most in the next 50); entering glyphs fade in over ~200 ms, no scale; exiting glyphs leave a 5–15 % ghost tail ~100–150 ms after the word reads as settled. A word is *extended* by sliding the prefix and fading the tail in, clipped by the pill edge while the pill widens (`Continue` → `Continue with Adjustments`). Adding an icon re-centres the whole label ~15–20 pt. When *every* character changes (a title, a full value), Family does not roll: a 150 ms crossfade with 0.93→1 scale and a touch of blur (51).
10. **Numbers count through intermediate values.** The odometer passes 0.11→0.13→0.14→0.15→0.16 at ~1 digit-step per 50 ms, so duration scales with distance (2–3 steps ≈ 150 ms, 5 steps ≈ 250 ms); digits that gain or lose a slot do so mid-roll and the **width tween lags one frame**; right-aligned strings grow leftward; separators roll (absent in the mid-frame, then in the new slot) and drop through the bottom clip on delete (39, 53, 55). Trend arrows rotate 180° in 150 ms ease-in-out; colour to green is immediate, to grey lags ~150 ms. Charts **morph** their y-values over 100–150 ms — the no-loader trick is that the old shape becomes the new one (53 vs 54).
11. **Page steps and tabs.** Linear steps: both layers travel ~120 pt (≈0.3 width) on **one** ease-out — no parallax (20, 24, 25) — outgoing opacity to 0 in ~100 ms, incoming in over 150 ms, title and content share the same offset (no stagger, 50); the ✕/‹ glyph swaps within one frame on release; the stepper hands off length first (100 ms) then colour (100–150 ms). A tray closes, the page rests ~200–250 ms, *then* the step starts (03, 49). Back steps are often pure in-place crossfades, even of the keyboard (49, 50). Tabs: 100–150 ms, incoming from the tapped side ~5–8 pt, outgoing drifting ~10–18 pt the other way, shared header pinned, icon filled on the first frame, a FAB that doesn't apply scales to ~0.3 in the same window (19, 25, 42).
12. **Shared elements lead by one frame.** The thing that carries meaning is ~75 % along its path in the first 50 ms; size and colour change *before* position (15, 16, 17, 46); paths are straight; context crossfades in place starting ~50 ms later; the layer being flown over (a keypad) drops out first (21, 33). Nothing that persists is duplicated, and it returns the way it came — except that "back" is frequently a crossfade rather than a reverse flight (49, 50).
13. **Loading keeps the layout and shows the wait as data.** Ghost value at ~30 % held (130–650 ms), then it **counts up** over 350–600 ms ease-in-out while brightening; the CTA enables ~100–150 ms after the count *starts*, not when it lands (11, 13, 28, 29, 35). Spinner→check is a 100 ms crossfade in the same slot. A skeleton with the exact geometry crossfades to real content in 100–150 ms (23). A masked value's shimmer is a 3-cell-wide soft band, ~100 ms per cell, +10 % lightness, one pass every ~1.7 s, first pass immediately (44). Old content is never blanked (54 is the counter-example).
14. **Screen-level swaps blur the outgoing layer.** Outgoing layer blurs, enlarges slightly (~1.05–1.07) and fades; incoming arrives sharp (41, 48). Zoom hierarchy: up a level = *both* layers shrink (incoming 1.07→1.0, outgoing 1.0→0.97); into a child = outgoing blurs and grows to ~1.06 (48). Blur is only ever a transition tool or a menu backdrop; nothing stays blurred.
15. **Delight follows the curve, and it is staged.** Rich, long sequences only on rare actions; they run as a chain (title → skeleton draws → traces → reveal → title → tooltip), each beat 150–550 ms, not everything at once. Frequent actions get the 100–150 ms flip. Illustrations wait ~300 ms after their tray lands, animate once (0.9–1.5 s), then hold still.
